# Script rules

The script is read aloud by a TTS voice and every word becomes a caption. Write for the ear, not the page.

## Length

Target word count = target seconds × 2.5 (about 150 words per minute). A 60s video is ~150 words, a 30s video ~75, a 3-minute video ~450. Stay within ±10%.

## Structure

1. **Hook (first 1–2 sentences, under 3 seconds spoken).** Open on the most surprising, specific, concrete thing about the topic. A claim, a number, an image. Never a greeting, never "In this video".
2. **Open loop.** Within the first ~5 seconds, set up a question the viewer needs answered, and hold the answer back.
3. **Body.** One idea per sentence. Every 2–3 sentences, something new: a fact, a turn, an escalation. Build toward the payoff; don't list.
4. **Payoff.** Close the open loop. Land it on the strongest line in the script.
5. **End clean.** One short closing line. No "thanks for watching", no "let me know in the comments" unless the user asks for a CTA.

## Engagement

- Specific beats general: "1,200 people" not "many people", "a Roman bathhouse" not "an old building".
- Use concrete nouns someone could picture, since every few seconds needs an image. A sentence with nothing to show is a weak sentence.
- Vary sentence length. Mostly short. Now and then a longer one that carries the viewer through a bit of story before snapping back.
- Talk to one person ("you"), in present tense where it works.
- Contractions always (it's, don't, they'd).

## Banned AI patterns — rewrite any line that has one

- **Negation-contrast:** "It's not X, it's Y", "This isn't just X — it's Y", "Not because X, but because Y". State Y directly.
- **Rule-of-three lists** of adjectives or phrases ("fast, cheap, and reliable") as a rhythm habit.
- **Em-dashes.** TTS reads them badly anyway. Use a full stop.
- **Rhetorical question chains** ("But why? What changed? And what does it mean for you?"). One question at most, and only as the open loop.
- **Stock openers/closers:** "Let's dive in", "Buckle up", "Here's the thing", "In today's world", "At the end of the day", "The truth is", "Imagine a world", "Picture this", "But here's the kicker", "And that's not all".
- **Inflated words:** delve, tapestry, testament, realm, landscape, navigate, unlock, unleash, elevate, game-changer, revolutionize, crucial, pivotal, vibrant, seamless, robust, journey (unless literal).
- **Summary tails:** "...and that's why X matters", "...which is a reminder that...". End on the fact, not the moral.
- **Hedging stacks:** "may potentially", "could arguably".
- **Colon reveals:** "The answer? Simple:".

## TTS hygiene

- Write numbers the way they should be said when it's ambiguous ("twenty twenty-four", "three point five million").
- No markdown, emoji, brackets, or stage directions in the spoken text. Anything on a line starting with `#` or `>` in `script.md` is skipped by the TTS step, so use those for titles/notes.
- Avoid abbreviations the voice may spell out (e.g. "approx.", "vs.").

## Self-check before showing the script

Read it back line by line against the banned list. Then ask: could a person who knows the topic well have said this out loud to a friend? If a line sounds like a brochure, rewrite it.
